## Slide 5 — Concepts (2)

- Knowledge
  - Information that is contextual, relevant, and actionable.
  - Understanding, awareness, or familiarity acquired through education or experience.
  - Anything that has been learned, perceived, discovered, inferred, or understood.
- In a knowledge management system, “knowledge is information in action”.

## Slide 6 — Concepts (3)

- Explicit Knowledge
  - Objective, rational, and technical material (data, policies, procedures, software, documents, etc.)
  - Easily documented, transferred, taught, and learned
  - E.g.: procedure, software, document
- Tacit knowledge
  - Subjective, cognitive, and experimental learning
  - Highly personal and difficulty to formalize
  - E.g.: individual’s belief, programming skill

## Slide 9 — KM & KMS

- Knowledge Management (KM)
  - Process of identify, capture, store, maintain, and deliver right knowledge to people within an organization.
- Knowledge Management System (KMS)
  - Application of technologies to assist knowledge management inside an organization.

## Slide 10 — Knowledge Development

- Process Approach
  - Attempts to codify organizational knowledge through formalized controls, processes and technologies.
  - Focuses on **explicit** knowledge and IT.
- Practice Approach
  - Focuses on building the social environments or communities of practice necessary to facilitate the sharing of tacit understanding.
  - Focuses on **tacit** knowledge and socialization.

## Slide 11 — Knowledge Development (2)

- Hybrid Approach
  - The practice approach is used so that a repository stores only explicit knowledge that is relatively easy to document.
  - Tacit knowledge initially stored in the repository is contact information about experts and their areas of expertise.
  - Increasing the amount of tacit knowledge over time eventually leads to the attainment of a true process approach.

## Slide 15 — Expert System

- A **knowledge-based information system** (KBIS) that uses its knowledge about a specific, complex application to act as an expert consultant to end users.
- KBIS is a system that adds a knowledge base to the other components on an IS.
- Goal – help nonexperts to make decisions and solve problems that usually require expertise.
- Works well in narrowly defined domains.

## Slide 16 — Expert System (2)

- Expert - A person who has the special knowledge, judgment, experience, and skills to provide sound advice and solve complex problems in a narrowly defined area.
- To be called an expert, one must be able to solve a problem and achieve a performance level that is significantly better than an average person.

## Slide 17 — Expert System (3)

- Expertise - The extensive, task-specific knowledge that experts possess.
- The level of expertise determines the success of a decision made by an expert.
- Expertise is often acquired through training, learning, and experience in practice.
- Expertise includes explicit knowledge, such as theories learned from a textbook or a classroom and implicit knowledge gained from experience.

## Slide 18 — Expert System (4)

- Main Components of ES
  - Knowledge acquisition (from humans and others)
  - Knowledge representation (if-then-else rules)
  - Knowledge base (knowledge repository)
    - Facts about specific subject area.
    - Heuristics that express the reasoning procedures of an expert (rules of thumb).
  - Inference engine (control/search structure)
    - Processes the knowledge and makes inferences to make recommend course of action.

## Slide 20 — Expert System (6)

- Pros
  - Faster and more consistent than an expert
  - Can have the knowledge of several experts
  - Does not get tired or distracted by overwork or stress
  - Helps preserve and reproduce the knowledge of experts
- Cons
  - Limited focus
  - Inability to learn
  - Maintenance problems
  - Developmental costs
  - Limited domain of knowledge

## Slide 21 — Intelligent Agents

- A software surrogate for an end user or a process that fulfills a stated need or activity.
- Uses its built-in and learned knowledge base.
- To make decisions and accomplish tasks in a way that fulfills the intentions of a user.
- Also called software robots or bots.

## Slide 22 — Intelligent Agents: Chatbots

- A computerized service that enables easy conversations between humans and humanlike computerized robots or image characters.
- Components
  - A person (client)
  - A computer, avatar, or robot (the AI machine)
  - A knowledge base (often stored at the cloud)
  - A human-computer interface (enabler of the dialog)
  - NLP that enables machine to “understand” the user

## Slide 24 — Recommender

- Recommendation system, also known as recommender system or recommendation engine.
- Recommending/suggesting one-to-one targeted products or services.
- Predict the importance (rating or preference) that a user will attach to a product or service.
  - Based on the prediction, specific products and services are recommended to the user.
  - Top applications include movies, music, and books.

However, there are also systems for travel, restaurants, insurance, and online dating.

## Slide 25 — Recommender (2)

- Benefits to customer:
  - Personalization
  - Discovery
  - Customer satisfaction
  - Reports
  - Increased dialog with seller
- Benefits to seller:
  - Higher conversion rate
  - Increased cross-sell
  - Increased customer loyalty
  - Enabling mass customization
