## Slide 10 — Decision Structure (2)

- **Structured**: Decision situations where the procedures to follow when a decision is needed can be specified in advance.
- **Unstructured**: Decision situations where it is not possible to specify in advance most of the decision procedures to follow.
- **Semi-structured**: Decision procedures that can be prespecified, but not enough to lead to a definite recommended decision.

## Slide 12 — Information Characteristics

**CONTENT** focuses on the **data** **itself** to ensure the quality, relevance, and completeness of the data.

**TIME** focuses on the **temporal aspects** of the data to ensure that data is up-to-date, timely, and appropriately frequent.

**FORM** focuses on the presentation and format of the data to ensure that the data can be interpreted easily.

## Slide 13 — Information Characteristics (2)

- **Time Dimension:** ! **Timeliness:** Data should be available when it is required.

! **Currency:** Data should be up-to-date.

! **Frequency:** Data should be updated based on needs.

! **Time period:** Data should cover the relevant the time span.

## Slide 14 — Information Characteristics (3)

- **Content Dimension:** ! **Accuracy:** Data must be correct.

! **Relevance:** Data must be useful for the decision-making.

! **Completeness:** Data should include all required data.

! **Conciseness:** Data should be succinct.

! **Scope:** Data should cover the necessary scope for analysis.

! **Performance**: Data should be processed in a way that maximizes its utility and accessibility.

## Slide 15 — Information Characteristics (4)

- **Form Dimension:** ! **Clarity:** Data should be presented in a clear manner.

! **Detail**: Data should provide the necessary levels of detail.

! **Order**: Data should be organized in a logical order.

! **Presentation**: Data should be visualized in a manner that could enhance comprehension.

! **Media**: Data should be accessible through appropriate media.

## Slide 19 — Management Information Systems (MIS)

- Produces information products that support many of the day-to-day decision-making needs of managers and business professionals.
- Prespecified reports, displays and responses.
- Support more structured decisions.

## Slide 20 — Decision Support Systems (DSS)

- Provide interactive information support to managers and business professionals during the decision- making process.
- Use: ! Analytical models.

! Specialized databases.

! A decision maker’s own insights and judgments.

- To support semi-structured business decisions.

## Slide 22 — Knowledge Management Systems (KMS)

- KMS are systems designed to capture, store, share, and manage the knowledge and information within an organization (**knowledge base**).
- A knowledge-based information system (KBIS) that uses its knowledge about a specific, complex application to act as an expert consultant to end users.
- KBIS is a system that adds a knowledge base to the other components on an IS.
