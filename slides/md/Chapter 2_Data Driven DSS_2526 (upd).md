## Slide 36 — Data Warehouse (DW)

“A **data warehouse (DW)** is a system that **extracts**, **cleans**, **conforms**, and **delivers** source data into a dimensional data store and then supports and implements querying and analysis for the purpose of decision making”

(**Ralph Kimball**)

***Building the Data Warehouse, 2005.***

“A **data warehouse (DW)** is a **subject -oriented**, **integrated**, **time-****variant**, and **nonvolatile** collection of data in support of managerial decision-making process”

**(W.H. Inmon)**

***The Data Warehouse ETL Toolkit, 2004.***

## Slide 37 — DW (2)

- **Characteristics of Data Warehouse:**
  - **Subject-oriented**: Data is organized *around key subjects* or themes relevant to the business, such as sales, products, or customers to support business decision-making.
  - **Integrated**: Data from different operational systems is *cleaned, transformed, and standardized*, ensuring uniformity in terms of naming conventions, data types, and formats.
  - **Time variant** (time series): Data is associated with specific *time intervals*, allowing for trend analysis, forecasting, and historical comparisons.
  - **Nonvolatile**: Data is only updated through periodic batch processes, which add new data but *do not alter existing* *records*.

- **Dimensional Approach**
  - **Star Schema**: A central **fact table** surrounded by several **dimension tables**.
  - **Snowflake Schema**: A more **complex version of a star** **schema** where dimension tables are normalized into multiple related tables.

## Slide 49 — Fact

- A fact **is a measurement of a business activity**, such as a business event or transaction, and is generally numeric

? Fact: sales

? Numeric measurements: counts, dollar amounts,

percentages, or ratios

- **Facts can be aggregated or derived**

? Ex: sum up the total revenue or calculate the profitability

of a set of sales transactions

- Facts provide the measurements of how well or how poorly the business is performing
- Fact tables are normalized and contain little redundancy

## Slide 50 — FACT TABLE

- Fact tables are composed of two types of columns:
  - **Keys**

◼ a group of foreign keys (FK) that point to the primary keys of dimensional tables

  - **Measures**

◼ actual measures of the business activity

◼ Every measurement has a grain, which is the level of detail in the measurement of an event

## Slide 51 — FACT TABLE KEYS

- **Key column**: consists of a group of foreign keys (FK) that point to the primary keys of dimensional tables that are associated with this fact table to enable business analysis.
- The primary key of a fact table is typically a multipart key consisting of the combination of foreign keys that can uniquely identify the fact table row.
  - primary key is a surrogate key.
  - primary key with degenerative dimensions

## Slide 52 — FACT TABLE MEASURES

Every measurement has a grain, which is the level of detail in the measurement of an event

Granularity is determined by its data source, indicates the level of detail stored in a table. Example:

- Each Time record represents a day
- Each Product record represents a product
- Each Organization record represents one branch
- then the grain of a sales Fact table with these dimensions is sales per product per day per branch

Granularity at **too high** a level severely limits the ability of users to obtain additional detail

Granularity at **too low** a level results in an exponential increase in the size requirements of the DW

## Slide 53 — FACT TABLE MEASURES

- **Type of fact measure**:
  - **Additive Facts**
    - can be summed across any of the dimensions associated with the fact table.
  - **Semi-additive Facts**
    - can be summed across some dimensions, but not all
  - **Non-additive Facts**
    - can’t be added across any dimensions

## Slide 57 — Advance modeling

**Types of Data Hierarchies:**

? **Balanced hierarchy**

◼ Every branch in the hierarchy has the same number of levels.

◼ All branches reach the same deepest level.

? **Unbalanced hierarchy**

◼ At least one branch does not reach the lowest level.

◼ Some branches have fewer levels than others.

**Ragged Hierarchy**

- Some branches skip one or more intermediate levels.
- Parent nodes may skip a level and link directly to a higher level.

## Slide 58 — Key Design Principles

- Define the grain first.
- Keep a consistent grain
  - Do not mix different grains in the same fact table.
- Validate the grain against business requirements
  - Can it support the required KPIs?
  - Can it support the required reports?
  - Does it provide the required level of drill-down?
- Revise the grain if necessary
  - If the required analyses cannot be supported, reconsider the grain.

## Slide 90 — Business Reporting

- A process of collecting, analyzing, and presenting data in a structured format, usually in the form of reports, to inform decision-making within an organization.
- These reports can be periodic (daily, weekly, monthly) or ad-hoc, and they typically include detailed information, analysis, and insights about specific business metrics or processes.

## Slide 91 — Ad-hoc Analysis

- **Multidimensional analysis** (OLAP operations)
  - **Slide/Dice**: Create a subset of cube data by selecting a range of values in one dimension (slice) or multiple dimensions (dice).
  - **Drill Down/Up**: Explore data hierarchies by moving to summary data (drill-up) or detailed data (drill-down).
  - **Roll-up**: Summarize detailed data into higher-level totals for strategic decision-making by aggregating data.
  - **Pivot (Rotate)**: Explore relationships between different dimensions by reorienting the cube.

## Slide 104 — Criteria for Handling Methods

- The nature of the missingness is important.
- Different contexts and types of analysis might require different treatments for missing values.
- Some default handling methods:
  - Missing values will be treated as 0 when summing data.
  - The sum will be equal to NaN, if all values are missing.
  - cumsum() and cumprod() methods ignore missing values but preserve them in the resulting arrays.
  - Missing values in GroupBy methods are excluded (like R).
  - In some descriptive statistics methods, the skipna option is to used if missing data should be excluded, i.e., this value is set to True by default (unlike R).

## Slide 106 — Outliers

## Slide 108 — IQR Method

- Steps for the IQR method:
  - Sort your data from low to high
  - Identify the values for Q1, Q2 (or the median), and Q3.
  - Calculate the IQR = Q3 – Q1
  - Calculate the upper fence (UF) = Q3 + (1.5 * IQR)
  - Calculate the lower fence (LF) = Q1 – (1.5 * IQR)
  - All values that fall outside the calculated fences (out of the range [LF, UF]) will be considered as outliers.

## Slide 109 — Z-Score Method

- Steps for the Z-Score Method
  - Convert extreme data points into z-scores.
  - All values that have a high enough or low enough z-scores (out of the range [-3𝜎, 3𝜎]) will be considered as outliers.
